# Local product readiness

The current delivery is the bounded single-device local-first edition. It does
not claim automatic AI execution of every function on arbitrary websites, an
unbreakable offline free limit, general customer deployment or public hosting.
Those limits must remain visible in the product and handoff.

## Agent completion sequence

1. Accept the integrated report workspace using exact-commit CI and installed
   browser evidence. Import seven existing local reviews without rewriting engine
   results or saved target settings. Keep old report bookmarks functional.
2. Fix the proven empty-root URL mismatch described in ROOT_URL_COMPATIBILITY.md;
   verify it on owned fixtures and the one specifically authorized read-only target.
3. Recheck the final installed interface, data preservation and current CI.
   Reuse accepted license, isolation, recovery and worker evidence; do not repeat
   unchanged external demo submissions or manufacture duplicate catalog cases.
4. Record final delivery evidence and a concise owner handoff. Leave Sprint 10
   closed and keep the usage-gated follow-up active until overall acceptance or
   cancellation.

## Owner-dependent actions after technical completion

1. Review the final local workspace and decide whether its delivered bounded scope
   and user experience are accepted. The owner controls overall product acceptance.
2. For testing a real authenticated customer workflow, supply an authorized test
   target and a disposable test account through local secret references. Never
   paste customer passwords into issues, reports or this repository. Unsupported
   authentication contracts still require scoped implementation; a credential alone
   does not make arbitrary login automation supported.
3. Before selling paid licenses, choose customer entitlement terms and keep the
   publisher signing key under owner control. Existing offline publisher tooling
   can generate/sign licenses locally; production private keys and trust anchors
   are not fabricated or committed as part of testing.
4. If a publicly hosted backend or a broader commercial distribution is desired,
   explicitly define that deployment scope and infrastructure. A purchased domain
   or server is not required to use the current local edition. No purchase or
   activation is made implicitly.
